Zeceipt · source of funds for shielded Zcash

Prove where your shielded ZEC came from, without handing over your viewing key.

An exchange, an OTC desk or a lender asks where your funds came from. Today the answers are to deshield first, or to hand over a viewing key that shows every payment you ever made. Zeceipt gives a third: a dossier of claims about the funds in question, each checked against the chain in the reviewer's browser.

How it works

  1. The reviewer sends a challenge

    The Case review page makes a fresh nonce. The holder answers it from their wallet: a small payment to themselves, with the nonce as the memo.

  2. The holder builds a dossier

    On the Build page, from their viewing key and the transactions of the funds. The key stays in the page; the dossier discloses only what its claims need.

  3. The reviewer checks every claim

    In their browser, against public nodes: a verdict per claim, a funds-flow timeline, and a report to file (PDF or JSON) with the dossier's hash.

Four kinds of claim

Origin
Where the funds entered the holder's wallet, and what funded that transaction.
Path
A disclosed note was spent in the transaction that created the next one.
Deposit
A payment the holder made, opened by a sender receipt, paid from disclosed notes.
Control
A challenge transaction spent disclosed notes and paid the holder a note whose memo carries the reviewer's nonce.

What a verified dossier shows

  • Each claim, checked against the transactions on chain: values, heights, and how the funds moved.
  • The transparent addresses that funded an origin, when there are any (they are public).
  • With a control answer: that the holder could spend the funds after your nonce was issued.

Honest limits

  • Who the counterparties are: an address that funded an origin is not a name.
  • The value of undisclosed inputs of a transaction: they are counted, not valued.
  • That funds are unspent now: control shows the holder could spend them when the challenge was made.
  • The dossier's nullifier key lets the reviewer see when a disclosed note is later spent; the dossier says so.
  • It is not a legal attestation: it is evidence a reviewer weighs.
